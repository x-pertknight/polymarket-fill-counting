Adding up every fill on a Polymarket market comes out about 10% above the money that moved. Reading the same fills wallet by wallet overstates it by about two thirds.

I compared the exchange's fill events with the cash in the transaction receipts for 60 markets that closed in October 2026.

Why the gap is small

Most matches are not a buyer meeting a seller. 71% are the exchange creating or destroying shares: two buyers fund a new pair of shares, or two sellers hand a pair back. There the fills add up to the cash once. Only 20% are a plain buyer-meets-seller trade, where the same cash shows up at both ends. I had predicted the opposite mix. 10 of my 21 scorable pre-registered predictions missed, most of them on this.

Two ways to add it up

Counting every fill at both ends is a choice of definition: about 10% above the cash that moved. Reading each fill under both wallets involved counts the same cash twice: 68% above.

What the venue reports

Take the volume Polymarket reports for a market after it closes. In about 20 of 35 markets it is within 5% of the fill total, and in about 20 within 5% of the cash moved. Where the two differ it sits between them. The data cannot tell which one it tracks. It is about 1.9 times the one-sided count (taker side only).

Limits

Figures are medians across markets. Markets opened after 1 September and closed in October. The transaction list came from the venue's API and was checked against the chain on 4 small and medium markets. 25 of the 60 markets, including most large ones, were read as a sample, so the venue comparison has only 2 large markets. The cash figure assumes the wallet named in a fill holds the money.
